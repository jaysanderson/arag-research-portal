# Research Portal - component video scripts

Eight short scripts, one per surface of the portal, aimed at roughly a minute each. Written for
the video producer working on the FRDC Knowledge Hub (Fisheries Research and Development
Corporation) build.

How to read each script:

- **On screen** - what to show and the moments worth highlighting.
- **Voiceover** - the narration, written for a research scientist as the viewer. It keeps the
  portal's own-system framing (it is "the research portal", not an "AI demo"), so the spoken track
  carries no vendor or AI branding.
- **Producer note** - background for you only, not for the voiceover: how Progress Agentic RAG
  provides the capability under the surface, so you understand what you are filming.

A note on the current build: the corpus enrichment pass has not been run yet, so some cards still
show raw project codes (for example `1989-109-DLD.pdf`) rather than merchandised titles and
summaries. If you are filming before that pass completes, favour the surfaces and moments that do
not lean on polished card titles, or note it for a re-shoot.

---

## 1. Explore - the way in

**On screen:** Land on the Explore home. Linger on the headline "What would you like to explore?"
and the ask box. Hover the suggested-question chips. Pan down to the figures row (resources,
paragraphs, sentences, index size), then scroll through a topic row (for example Carp Control) and
its cards. Finish by clicking a suggested question so it hands off to a live answer.

**Voiceover:** This is the front door to the whole body of research. You do not need to know the
right keywords to start. Ask a question in plain language, or pick one of the suggested questions
drawn straight from the corpus itself. The figures underneath give you an honest sense of the
scale you are working with: how many reports and submissions sit behind the portal, and how deep
the index goes. Below that, the topic rows show what the collection actually covers, each with a
handful of representative documents, so you can browse in when you are not sure what to search
for. Pick a question and the portal takes it straight to a grounded, cited answer. One surface,
two ways in: ask, or explore.

**Producer note:** The suggested questions and the topic rows are generated by the platform's
classification index over the connected knowledge box, not a hand-written menu, so they reflect
the real content. Clicking a question hands it to the same grounded answer engine the Assistant
uses.

## 2. Search - results, or a cited answer

**On screen:** Type "fish habitat". Show the two buttons, Search and Ask AI. Click Search first:
results appear with a relevance percentage and the matched passage on each. Toggle Hybrid /
Semantic / Keyword, open Filters, and flick Match strength from All to Strong. Point out
"Summarise these results" and "Watch this search". Then click Ask AI and let the short cited
answer write itself over the same results.

**Voiceover:** Search gives you two jobs from one box. Press Search and you get a ranked list of
matching passages, fast and answer-free, each carrying the exact text that matched and a calibrated
relevance score, so you can judge a source before you open it. A weak match looks weak, and a
question the collection cannot answer honestly returns nothing rather than bluffing. Choose how
matching works, filter by topic or document type, or show only the strong matches. When you would
rather have the thread pulled together for you, press Ask AI: it reads the top results and writes a
short answer with every claim cited back to its source. And you can watch a search, so the portal
re-checks it for you each day and flags anything new.

**Producer note:** Search runs hybrid retrieval with the platform's reranker; the percentage is a
calibrated relevance score, not a raw distance. Ask AI is the grounded, cited RAG answer over the
retrieved set. "Watch this search" is a stored search the platform re-runs on a schedule.

## 3. Library - the full catalogue

**On screen:** Open the Library on the full grid (hundreds of resources). Open the sort menu and
show Newest added, Oldest added, Title A-Z. Use the Topics list on the left to narrow to one area.
Type into "Search within the library" and show real results, not just a title match. Scroll to
show the endless-but-fast paging.

**Voiceover:** The Library is the complete catalogue of everything connected to the portal. Sort it
by what is newest, oldest, or by title, and page through a large collection without waiting for the
whole thing to load. Narrow it to a single area of research from the topics on the left. And when
you search within the Library, you are not doing a weak match on file names: it uses the same
retrieval engine as Search, so it finds the documents a plain title filter would miss. Failed
imports and junk pages are kept out automatically, so what you browse is genuine research, and each
document is meant to read as real work rather than a raw file name.

**Producer note:** In-library search is retrieval-backed, not a string filter. The hiding of
failed and junk items is the platform's displayable-resource filtering. The intended card
presentation (title, summary, key takeaways) comes from the platform's document-augmentation
merchandising fields, which is the enrichment pass mentioned at the top of this document.

## 4. Assistant - a research conversation with memory

**On screen:** Open the Assistant with its session sidebar. Ask a real question and let the answer
stream: show the stage line (retrieving, writing, checking), the "Interpreted as..." line, then the
cited answer. Highlight the confidence headline and the answer-quality meters directly under the
answer. Click to open the sources and evidence, then the "Journey through the context". Toggle Deep
research on. Finish on Export.

**Voiceover:** The Assistant is a conversation with the whole collection, and it remembers where you
have been, so you can keep drilling in without starting over. Ask a question and watch it work:
first it shows you how it read your question, then it retrieves, writes, and checks. Every answer is
cited, claim by claim, back to the passage that supports it. Right under the answer you get a plain
read on how far to trust it, with relevance, groundedness and context scored openly. The evidence
sits one click away when you want to audit it, source by source. For a broad or multi-part question,
turn on Deep research and the portal breaks it into focused sub-questions and reads the full
documents. When you are done, export the whole trail.

**Producer note:** This is streamed RAG with claim-level citations. The three quality scores are the
platform's REMi trust signal (answer relevance, groundedness, context relevance). Deep research
decomposes the question into sub-questions and switches to full-document grounding. Each evidence
row also carries an advisory AI verdict on whether that source supports the answer.

## 5. Investigations - turn a question into a living case

**On screen:** Create an investigation with a name and a research question. Show evidence saved into
it (from Search or the Assistant), each with its "Retrieved for..." provenance. Triage a couple of
items with Supports / Partial / Contradicts / Not relevant, then use the tabs to filter. Add a tag
and a note. Show the Notebook. Finish on "Synthesise the evidence" and Export to Word.

**Voiceover:** An investigation is for the question you are actually trying to answer, the one that
takes more than a single search. Give it a name, then gather evidence into it as you work, from
search, from the assistant, from any document. Each piece keeps its provenance, so you always know
where it came from and what you were asking when you found it. Weigh each one: does it support the
case, partly, or contradict it? The tabs then let you see just the supporting or just the
conflicting evidence at a glance. Tag evidence under the claims you are testing, keep your working
notes in the notebook, and when you are ready, synthesise across everything you have kept, grounded
strictly on your own evidence and nothing else. Then export the whole case.

**Producer note:** Evidence persists per investigation. The verdict chips are the same platform
judge used in the Assistant's evidence table. Synthesis is grounded only on the collected evidence,
not the whole corpus, which is the point of difference from a normal ask.

## 6. Generate - structured artefacts from the corpus

**On screen:** Open Generate. Show the six tabs: Comparison, Briefing, Timeline, Pros and cons,
FAQ, Assessment. Pick Comparison, use a suggested chip, and generate. Show the finished artefact
with its sources. If you can trigger it, show the grounding gate declining a topic the corpus does
not cover. Finish on export.

**Voiceover:** Generate turns the collection into something you can hand on. Choose the shape you
need: a side-by-side comparison, a briefing, a timeline, a pros-and-cons, an FAQ, or an assessment.
Describe your topic, or start from a suggestion, and the portal drafts it to that structure, built
on real retrieved content rather than invented from thin air. If the collection does not hold enough
to support the request, it tells you so and declines, rather than producing something plausible but
unsupported. Every artefact carries its sources with it, and exports straight to a Word document, so
it drops into your own work.

**Producer note:** Generation is schema-constrained and grounded in retrieval. The "grounding gate"
is a deliberate refusal when retrieval is too thin, so nothing is fabricated. When grounding is
partial, only the genuinely supporting sources are cited.

## 7. Assessment - test what you know

**On screen:** Open Assessment on the knowledge-area cards (each showing its source count). Pick one,
then show the two choices: number of questions (3, 5 or 10) and depth (Foundational, Intermediate,
Advanced). Generate the check, then show a couple of questions being answered and the result with
sources to read up on.

**Voiceover:** Assessment is a quick way to test your grasp of an area, or to bring someone new up to
speed. Pick a knowledge area, choose how many questions you want and how deep to go, from
foundational through to advanced, and the portal writes a knowledge check from the sources in that
area. Work through it, and see how you did, with the relevant documents to read up on anything you
missed. Because the questions come from the collection itself, you are testing understanding of the
real material, not trivia.

**Producer note:** The questions are generated from the corpus for the chosen topic and depth, and
are grounded so each traces back to source material. This is the same grounded-generation capability
as Generate, pointed at a self-check.

## 8. Graph - the shape of the whole collection

**On screen:** Open the Graph (titled the Knowledge map). Start on the Entity graph. Show the nodes
and the legend (species, regions, programs, habitats, technologies), the "Most connected" list, and
"Find in the map". Click a node to open its evidence. Flick the "Include built-in entities" toggle.
Then switch to the Concept map tab and show the themes drawing together.

**Voiceover:** The Knowledge map lets you see the whole collection at once. The entity graph shows
the things the research is actually about, the species, places, programs and technologies, joined by
the relationships found between them across every document. Follow the most connected entities, find
one by name, or click any node to jump to the evidence behind it. Turn on the built-in entities for
a fuller, noisier picture that adds people, dates and places. And when you want to step back to the
level of themes, the concept map shows how the broad areas of research overlap, so you can see where
different fields meet. It is a map you explore, drawn from the content itself, not a diagram someone
drew by hand.

**Producer note:** The entities and relations come from the platform's knowledge-graph extraction
agent. "Include built-in entities" adds the platform's raw named-entity extraction. The concept map
is built from classification co-occurrence across the corpus. Drag to pan, scroll to zoom, click a
node to explore.

---

### Running order suggestion

If the eight are cut into one reel, a natural order is: Explore, Search, Library, Assistant,
Investigations, Generate, Assessment, Graph - front door first, then find, then browse, then the
deeper research tools, closing on the map. Each stands alone at about a minute, so they can also
ship as standalone clips.
